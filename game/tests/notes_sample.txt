The French Revolution began in 1789 and ended in the late 1790s. It started because France faced a severe financial crisis after expensive wars, including support for the American Revolution. King Louis XVI called the Estates-General in May 1789 to raise taxes. The Third Estate, representing commoners, declared itself the National Assembly in June 1789. On 14 July 1789, crowds stormed the Bastille, a royal fortress and prison in Paris. The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen was adopted in August 1789 and stated that men are born free and equal in rights. In 1792 France was declared a republic and the monarchy was abolished. Louis XVI was executed by guillotine in January 1793. The period from 1793 to 1794 is known as the Reign of Terror, led by Maximilien Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety. During the Terror, thousands of suspected enemies of the revolution were executed. Robespierre himself was executed in July 1794. The Directory governed France from 1795 until 1799. In November 1799, Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in a coup and became First Consul.
